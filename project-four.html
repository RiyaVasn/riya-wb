Wind Powered Lighting & Charging Units

Autonomous Highway Infrastructure powered by Maglev VAWTs and Solar Microgrids

An integrated, off-grid solution designed to place autonomous lighting and charging units every 20 km along highways. By capturing traffic-induced wind turbulence and solar energy, the system independently powers smart lighting and provides scalable EV charging.

Tools & Methods

ANSYS Fluent SolidWorks Maglev Tribology Systems Integration CFD Simulation Structural FEA V2G / G2V Microgrids MBSE

The Autonomous Infrastructure Challenge

Modern highway networks consume substantial energy for illumination, while the growth of electric vehicles is limited by gaps in rural charging access. To reduce grid dependency, this project uses a Model-Based Systems Engineering (MBSE) approach to design a self-sustaining assembly that combines a 1.6 m² gallium arsenide solar panel with a high-efficiency vertical-axis wind turbine.

Systems Architecture & MBSE

To coordinate the mechanical, electrical, and environmental subsystems, a top-down Model-Based Systems Engineering (MBSE) architecture defined the structural analysis requirements, turbine-blade optimization goals, and material selections linking the Savonius rotor to the microgrid charging units.

Aerodynamics & Viscous Flow CFD

To operate in the irregular wind profiles generated by moving vehicles, the design uses a Savonius-type vertical-axis wind turbine (VAWT), selected for its self-starting ability and lack of a complex yaw mechanism. Fluid flow was simulated in ANSYS Fluent using a dynamic moving mesh and a K-Epsilon SST turbulence model to resolve the viscous sublayer and evaluate wall shear stress.

Structural Dynamics & Magnetic Levitation

To reduce the start-up friction of mechanical bearings in low-speed environments, a neodymium magnetic levitation (Maglev) suspension was integrated. Two large ring magnets and evenly spaced permanent magnets create a repulsive suspension gap for the rotor. Dynamic finite element analysis (FEA) was performed on the pole and turbine to extract the first six natural frequencies and check the structure against resonance from traffic turbulence.

Power Electronics & Microgrid Integration

An intelligent electro-mechanical relay system acts as the central logic controller, switching the primary input source between the turbine and the solar array based on environmental availability. The output features a dual-topology microgrid supporting Vehicle-to-Grid (V2G) and Grid-to-Vehicle (G2V) charging, utilizing boost converters and LC filters to safely regulate high-voltage delivery to a 100Ah Lithium-ion EV battery bank.

Economic Feasibility & Cost Analysis

A comprehensive cost analysis was conducted to validate the commercial viability of the system for real-world application. Through consultation with industry mentors and supplier brochures, the total fabrication budget was calculated to include the turbine (€1,030), 60W solar panels (€260), integration with existing street light structures (€194), and the complete EV charging battery and wiring system (€85).
