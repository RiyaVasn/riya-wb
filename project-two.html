The Aerodynamic Challenge

The aviation industry requires drastic reductions in fuel consumption and aerodynamic drag. Blended Wing Body designs integrate the fuselage and wings to minimize wetted surface area, reducing fuel burn by up to 50 percent. Optimizing these geometries while maintaining airport compatibility remains a critical challenge.

Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) Simulation

I developed a 3D hybrid body aircraft model using a blended NACA 4414 airfoil and analyzed it in ANSYS Fluent. Using the Reynolds Averaged Navier Stokes procedure, I simulated cruising conditions at 150 m/s and 10,000 meters altitude. Second order formulations for pressure, density, momentum, and energy ensured high computational accuracy.

Statistical Optimization (RSM & ANOVA)

To avoid expensive brute force simulations, I applied a Design of Experiments methodology. Using Response Surface Methodology and Central Composite Design, I mapped the effects of frontal area, angle of attack, and material composition. Analysis of Variance quantified their exact impact on the drag coefficient. This generated a regression model matching CFD results with less than a 5 percent error margin.

Advanced Materials for Lightweighting

Drag reduction relies heavily on lightweighting. I investigated Shape Memory Polymers integrated with nanofillers as an alternative to traditional aluminum. The analysis proved that a composite of 45 percent Carbon Fiber, 45 percent Glass Fiber, and 10 percent Graphene Nano Powder by weight yielded the minimum drag coefficient.

Engineering Impact & Final Results

The optimization pinpointed the exact parameters to maximize flight efficiency. The lowest drag coefficient of 0.018 was achieved at a frontal area of 1625 square meters, a negative 10 degree angle of attack, and the graphene enhanced composite blend. This research provides a scalable framework combining CAD, CFD, and statistical analysis for hybrid aircraft design.

  • Wind Energy: CFD optimization for turbine blade efficiency.
